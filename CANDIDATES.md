# Candidates & tournament — Museum of the Year 3000

Judge brief (criteria in weight order, from the published rules):

1. On-subject: the museum label a museum in the year 3000 puts beside an
   ordinary object from today — not a caption about something else.
2. The joke lands on a first read, with the laugh in the line itself.
3. The humour comes from the year 3000 misreading the object — the label says
   something true and absurd about what the thing was for.
4. Reads like a real museum label: short, straight-faced, confident, one line,
   no throat clearing.
5. The object and the angle are fresh, not the obvious first joke about the
   obvious first object.

A label that explains its own joke is weaker than one that trusts the reader
to get it. The brief's own examples (plastic fork, parking meter, house key,
group chat) were deliberately avoided as objects, since the obvious first joke
about them is the weakest play.

## Tournament bracket

Each match was decided by the brief's criteria in weight order: the first
criterion where the two differ decides, unless a later one differs by much
more.

### Round 1

- **M1: C1 beats C2.** Both land, but C1's misreading is total — the
  toothbrush's purpose reframed as scrubbing the bones in one's mouth — while
  C2's alarm genuinely does scream at smoke, so its misreading is thinner
  (criterion 3).
- **M2: C3 beats C4.** C4 is elegant, but C3's laugh is bigger and lands on
  the first read: a road to nowhere, walked by people who owned cars
  (criterion 2).
- **M3: C5 beats C6.** Both misread well; C5's "doors, menus and debts"
  kicker is the harder first-read laugh (criterion 2).
- **M4: C7 beats C8.** C7 is one clean misreading — the shower as rain kept
  indoors — shorter and more confident as a label; C8's traffic light is the
  more obvious object (criteria 4 and 5).
- **M5: C9 beats C10.** C10 is dry and wry, but C9 misreads deeper — a roof
  you carry around for when the sky falls (criterion 3).
- **M6: C12 beats C11.** C11 is a neat image; C12's inversion — humans
  walked by their smaller masters — is the bigger laugh and the fresher angle
  (criteria 2 and 5).
- **M7: C13 beats C14.** C14's pine forest is a good misreading, but C13's
  "the page where the reader gave up" kicker lands harder (criterion 2).
- **M8: C15 beats C16.** C16's "sacred waters" is funny, but C15's misreading
  is richer: an all-seeing eye that visitors spoke their names to
  (criterion 3).

### Quarter-finals

- **Q1: C3 beats C1.** Two strong misreadings; C3's "endless road to nowhere"
  plus the car-owner kicker is the bigger first-read laugh (criterion 2).
- **Q2: C7 beats C5.** C5's "debts" kicker is good, but C7 is the shorter,
  cleaner, more confident label with a single laugh (criteria 2 and 4).
- **Q3: C12 beats C9.** The dog-lead inversion out-laughs the portable roof
  (criteria 2 and 5).
- **Q4: C15 beats C13.** C13's "gave up" is dry and funny, but C15 misreads
  the object more deeply (criterion 3).

### Semi-finals

- **S1: C3 beats C7.** C7 is elegant and compact, but C3's laugh is bigger
  and more immediate on a first read (criterion 2).
- **S2: C12 beats C15.** C15 is the deeper misreading, but C12's "smaller
  masters" kicker is the bigger first-read laugh, and criterion 2 is weighed
  before criterion 3's margin here is decisive (criterion 2).

### Final

- **F: C3 beats C12.** Both are on-subject, single-line, straight-faced
  labels. C12 has the fresher angle (criterion 5: the power inversion is less
  familiar than treadmill jokes). But the treadmill's laugh is the bigger one
  on a first read — "an endless road to nowhere" lands instantly, and "walked
  daily by people who owned cars" is a true-and-absurd kicker about what the
  thing was for — and criterion 2 is weighed before criterion 5. The final
  was close; C12 is the strongest runner-up.

## Champion

**C3 — Treadmill, c. 2023. An endless road to nowhere, walked daily by people who owned cars. (86 chars)**

It trusts the reader to get it: no explanation, no throat clearing, one
confident line.
